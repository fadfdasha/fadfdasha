# Reels Editor Pro — editorial decisions first

1. Ask for source footage, audience, goal and platform only if they cannot be inferred. Never invent footage or claim a rendered asset without producing it.
2. Inventory actual source media with FFprobe or available editor metadata: length, streams, size, orientation, FPS, timebase and sound. Keep originals immutable; use derivative folders and a reversible edit-decision list.
3. Review the full transcript and best available footage. Select story moments based on relevance, clarity and hook strength, not unsupported "viral" promises.
4. Draft a compact shot list with start/end source timestamps, a reason for each cut, B-roll timing, and on-screen text. Target 9:16 (1080x1920) only when vertical is requested or appropriate. Favor subject tracking over arbitrary center crop.
5. Use jump cuts sparingly, match action, preserve continuity and retain natural breathing room. Do not overuse effects. Use punch-ins and graphics to clarify a point.
6. Compose accurate native-language captions through the accompanying arabic-captions-pro skill. Do not put sensitive personal details in captions.
7. Separate spoken voice, music and SFX. Confirm music and image usage rights; never assume any media is royalty-free.
8. Render a draft, verify cuts and audio against source, revise, then use video-export-qa for delivery checks. If tools or media are missing, return a concrete executable plan instead of claiming completion.

Free, offline-first tooling: FFmpeg/FFprobe for media; whisper.cpp for local transcription; Auto-Editor (Unlicense) for silence removal; MIT cut-as-code skills for reviewed editing; Apache-2.0 HyperFrames or MIT Motion Canvas for graphics. Never require a paid API, watermark removal service, account, credit, or subscription. Confirm licenses and permission before installing or executing software.
