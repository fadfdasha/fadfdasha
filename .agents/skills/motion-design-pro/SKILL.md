# Motion Design Pro

1. Clarify scene purpose and delivery format from the brief. Build a short storyboard, typography hierarchy, motion beats, brand palette and asset list.
2. If an existing project is provided, inspect its timeline/components first and reuse existing patterns. Do not replace working rendering infrastructure unnecessarily.
3. Prefer MIT Motion Canvas for TypeScript motion graphics or Apache-2.0 HyperFrames for HTML/CSS graphics. Keep free/open-source workflows by default; no paid rendering APIs or mandatory subscriptions. Do not replace an existing renderer without compatibility testing or mix renderers without technical justification.
4. Animate for meaning: intentional entrances/exits, easing, hierarchy, readable pauses, consistent timing and restrained transitions; avoid flashes or sensory overload.
5. Verify infographic facts and cite source material outside the video where appropriate. Do not fabricate stats.
6. Maintain safe areas in vertical 9:16, preserve editable layers, avoid copyright-infringing music or unlicensed image assets.
7. Preview decisive frames and motion at target FPS; review Arabic glyph shaping, line breaks, z-order, audio sync and compositing errors.
8. Export with video-export-qa and clearly state which frames, tools and export path were actually checked.

Licenses: Motion Canvas MIT; HyperFrames Apache-2.0. Both frameworks are free to use as provided, but verify transitive dependencies, third-party media and font licensing before distributing a product.
